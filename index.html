About

I am a Research Scientist at the MIT-IBM Watson AI Lab, IBM Research, where I work on the architecture of large language models — specifically on what happens when you stop treating a transformer as a feedforward stack and start treating it as a dynamical system with an energy function.

That reframing is not cosmetic. It gives inference a stopping criterion, gives depth a meaning beyond "how many blocks we could afford," and gives expert routing in Mixture-of-Experts models a principled objective instead of a hand-tuned auxiliary loss. Our ICLR 2026 paper, NRGPT, is the first full language model built this way; the follow-on work asks how much compute you can save when a model is allowed to decide for itself that it has converged.

Before this I completed my PhD in Computer Science at Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute with Mohammed J. Zaki, where I worked on making discrete optimization differentiable by routing it through associative memory dynamics. Before graduate school I spent roughly three years building production systems — most of it at bKash, the mobile financial platform that moves money for more than 65 million people in Bangladesh. That period shapes how I evaluate research: I am persistently interested in whether a method survives contact with real scale.

Always glad to talk about energy-based architectures and associative memory — and happy to give a seminar or reading-group talk on this work. Get in touch.

What I work on

01

Energy-based language models

Rewriting the transformer forward pass as gradient descent on a scalar energy. The model has a well-defined objective at inference time, and the number of steps it takes becomes an observable rather than a hyperparameter.

02

Associative memory

Modern Hopfield networks as a computational primitive. Attention is one instance; so is clustering, so is retrieval. I am interested in what else becomes differentiable once you express it as relaxation toward a stored pattern.

03

Sparse routing & test-time compute

Deriving Mixture-of-Experts routing from a free-energy functional instead of bolting on a load-balancing penalty, and letting a model halt when its energy has converged rather than after a fixed budget.

Selected publications

ICLR — main conference 2026 Co-first author

NRGPT: An Energy-based Alternative for GPT

Nima Dehmamy*, Benjamin Hoover*, Bishwajit Saha*, Leo Kozachkov, Jean-Jacques Slotine, Dmitry Krotov*

A language model whose forward pass minimises a scalar energy rather than executing a fixed layer stack, unifying autoregressive generation with associative-memory dynamics. Holds up across tasks from algebraic reasoning to open-domain generation.

Adaptive Test-Time Compute with Energy Transformer Modules

        
Bharat Runwal, Bishwajit Saha, Benjamin Hoover, Dmitry Krotov, Mauro Martino, Nima Dehmamy

        
A nine-architecture factorial study at 400M–1B scale separating two recurrent-transformer computation modes — distributed refinement under energy attention versus concentrated construction under standard attention — giving 12.8–19% FLOP savings from adaptive halting at no quality cost.

Boltzmann Routing for Energy-Compatible Mixture of Experts

        
Bharat Runwal, Bishwajit Saha, Benjamin Hoover, Mauro Martino, Nima Dehmamy

        
An auxiliary-loss-free routing method derived from a free-energy functional, with a proof of exact energy compatibility and a formal attention–routing duality inside Energy Transformers.

Deep Clustering with Associative Memories

End-to-end Differentiable Clustering with Associative Memories

        
Bishwajit Saha, Dmitry Krotov, Mohammed J. Zaki, Parikshit Ram
